# Pre-Slip Reachable-Set Dominance: Bounded Theory Certificate

Date: 2026-07-10

## Status and claim boundary

This document defines the theoretical half of the proposed dual proof for the
AutoDrift C5' paper. It is a bounded control-theoretic certificate, not a claim
about every vehicle, road, obstacle, or terminal-pose objective.

The target statement is:

> For a vehicle starting in the pre-slip region, deliberate high-sideslip
> operation does not enlarge the collision-avoidance feasible set when the
> pre-slip and high-slip arms share the same tire, actuator, disturbance, and
> geometric constraints and high slip unlocks no additional actuator. For an
> already-sliding state, enlarging the available control set weakly enlarges the
> recovery set; strict enlargement is conditional on a matched witness and is
> not implied merely by the presence of slip.

The first sentence is proved below for a force-envelope differential-inclusion
model. The second sentence is a set-nesting proposition, not a strictness
theorem. Detailed single-track and Chrono models are used to try to falsify the
assumptions and find a strict witness. A finite experiment cannot turn the
bounded theorem into a universal vehicle theorem.

## Definitions

Let `x` be the chassis state: planar pose, body velocity, and yaw rate. The
theorem below is posed at the force-envelope level: body-frame tire forces are
the directly selected inputs. A force-command filter state may be included when
its evolution depends only on that selected force history, but steering-angle,
wheel-speed, and tire-relaxation realization are not asserted by this model.

For tire or axle `i`, at fixed normal load `Fz_i` and longitudinal force `Fx_i`,
let

- `G_i(Fz_i, Fx_i)` be the closure of body-frame force vectors available on
  the ascending/peak branch of the lateral force-slip curve;
- `S_i(Fz_i, Fx_i)` be the body-frame force vectors available on the high-slip
  branch;
- `W(F_1, ..., F_n)` be the net body wrench generated by the tire forces.

For horizon `T`, obstacle set `O`, and initial-state set `X0`, define

- `K_G(T, O)` as the initial states from which an admissible pre-slip force
  selection yields a collision-free trajectory through `T`;
- `K_S(T, O)` as the initial states from which a branch-labelled force selection
  required to use the high-slip branch yields a collision-free trajectory
  through `T`. The branch label is not part of the chassis state.

The load-bearing claim is `K_S(T, O) subseteq K_G(T, O)`, equivalently that the
drift-only set `K_S \ K_G` is empty, under the assumptions below.

## Assumptions

- **A1, matched chassis:** both arms use the same mass, inertia, geometry,
  tire law, road friction, and disturbance set.
- **A2, pre-slip initial state:** every tire starts on the ascending/peak force
  branch. The theorem does not apply to an initially developed slide.
- **A3, branch force inclusion:** for every admitted `(Fz_i, Fx_i)`,
  `S_i(Fz_i, Fx_i) subseteq G_i(Fz_i, Fx_i)`.
- **A4, force-envelope input model:** tire-force vectors are measurable direct
  selections. Any force-command magnitude, slew, or delay restriction is
  identical and branch-independent, so the same force history is admissible in
  either arm. This assumption does not grant steering-angle realization.
- **A5, no slide-only actuator:** rear steering, direct yaw moment, differential
  wheel torque, or another actuator is not enabled only for the high-slip arm.
- **A6, common state constraints:** road boundaries, collision geometry,
  progress requirements, and terminal constraints are identical.
- **A7, static uniform avoidance objective:** the obstacle is static, friction
  is scalar and uniform, and no terminal heading or pose is required beyond
  collision-free passage and the common road/progress constraints. Moving
  obstacles and split-mu are outside scope.
- **A8, well-posed dynamics:** measurable force selections generate unique
  absolutely continuous state trajectories over the finite horizon.

Assumption A3 is satisfied in this force-envelope abstraction by a continuous
single-peaked force-slip curve when the post-peak force magnitude never exceeds
the peak: every post-peak force value belongs to the ascending-branch image by
the intermediate value theorem. A friction ellipse does not create extra
high-slip force when deliberate power-oversteer or braking spends longitudinal
capacity, because the matched pre-slip arm retains the same `(Fx_i, Fy_i)`
choice. A3 alone does **not** prove that a steering-angle controller can produce
that choice under transient wheel and tire kinematics; A4 deliberately marks
that realization boundary.

## Theorem 1: force-envelope reachable-set inclusion

Under A1-A8,

`K_S(T, O) subseteq K_G(T, O)` for every finite horizon `T` and admitted
obstacle set `O`.

### Proof

Take any `x0` in `K_S(T, O)`. By definition there is a measurable branch-labelled
tire-force history `F_i^S(t)` that uses the required high-slip mode and whose trajectory
is collision-free through `T`. Before high-slip entry its force vectors already
belong to `G_i`; during high slip A3 says the same vectors also belong to
`G_i`. Therefore define `F_i^G(t) := F_i^S(t)` for almost every `t`. A4 makes
this identical force history admissible for the pre-slip envelope, and A1/A5
give

`W(F_1^G(t), ..., F_n^G(t)) = W(F_1^S(t), ..., F_n^S(t))`.

The two selections therefore induce the same chassis-state derivative at the
same state. By A8 and the common initial state, their state trajectories are
equal almost everywhere and hence everywhere by continuity. A6-A7 give the same
collision, road, progress, and terminal outcomes. Thus the matched pre-slip
selection is feasible and `x0` is in `K_G(T, O)`. Since `x0` was arbitrary,
`K_S(T, O) subseteq K_G(T, O)`. QED.

This proof is constructive only for the stated force-input differential
inclusion. It does not assert that a steering-angle controller can realize the
matching force history under arbitrary transient wheel and tire kinematics.
That realization gap is the target of the detailed-model falsification and
Chrono experiment; it is not hidden inside the theorem.

## Lemma 1: lane-aligned rectangular footprint

For a rectangular vehicle with half-length `l`, half-width `w`, and `l >= w`,
the lateral half-width of its projection at yaw angle `psi` is

`h_y(psi) = l |sin(psi)| + w |cos(psi)|`.

For `|psi| <= pi/2`, `h_y(psi) >= w = h_y(0)`. Therefore yawing the body cannot
reduce the lateral swept footprint against a lane-aligned obstacle. It may
increase it through tail swing. This lemma does not cover arbitrary terminal
pose requirements or a corridor whose useful axis is itself rotated.

## Finite-domain strict boundary criterion

The continuous theorem gives weak inclusion. A strict numerical claim needs a
separate, explicitly restricted witness. Let `L` be a frozen finite action
library and `D` a frozen finite obstacle-distance grid. For a matched cell and
seed define

`D_G* = min {d in D : some action in L clears under the grip predicate}`

and define `D_S*` analogously for the required-slide predicate. Smaller is
better. If both are finite and `D_G* < D_S*`, then the scenario at `D_G*` is a
grip-only witness on `(L, D)`: a grip action clears there, while no action in the
same library clears there under the required-slide predicate. This proves a
strict boundary gap on that finite domain. It does not establish strict
containment of the continuous detailed-model kernels.

## Proposition 1: weak post-slip recovery inclusion and strictness criterion

Let `U_B` be a baseline post-slip control set and `U_A` be an expanded set that
permits every action in `U_B`. For the same plant, initial state, horizon, and
recovery target, the kernels satisfy `R_B subseteq R_A`. The inclusion is
strict if and only if there is at least one matched initial state in
`R_A \ R_B`. A sufficient numerical witness is an added-control trajectory
that recovers by time `T` while every baseline trajectory fails by `T`.

The set inclusion follows directly from `U_B subseteq U_A`. Strictness is not
obtained from set algebra alone; it requires a matched witness. If the earliest
expanded recovery time is at least `Delta t` earlier than the earliest baseline
time, every deadline in the resulting open interval also gives a strict finite-
horizon witness. Neither slip magnitude nor the fact that steering authority is
available proves that such an interval is nonempty.

The old `scripts/audits/chrono_recovery.py` and
`scripts/audits/recovery_reachability.py` counts are invalid for this purpose:
they treated normalized pedal `0` as physical zero, whereas this repository's
model contract maps physical zero to normalized `-1`. Uniform service braking
also must not be labeled production ESC because no individual-wheel yaw-moment
authority is modeled.

## Required positive control and known counterexample condition

Zhao, Yurtsever, and Rizzoni, *Justifying Emergency Drift Control for
Automated Vehicles*, IFAC-PapersOnLine 55(24), 2022,
doi:10.1016/j.ifacol.2022.10.275, constructs a conventional Dubins
justification model with `|yaw_rate| <= 0.20 rad/s` and a beyond-limit model
with `|yaw_rate| <= 0.26 rad/s`. The latter control set is strictly larger, so
the paper exhibits a close-obstacle case that the first model cannot avoid and
the second can.

That result does not contradict Theorem 1 because A4 and A5 are intentionally
violated: the two arms have different admissible yaw-rate sets. The new
experiment must reproduce this case as a positive control. Failure to recover
the known drift-only witness invalidates a later no-witness conclusion.

## What would falsify the AutoDrift theorem bridge

Any of the following blocks the detailed-model claim while leaving the bounded
force-envelope theorem intact:

1. A matched-budget, matched-actuator validation cell in which a required-slide
   trajectory clears and the best pre-slip oracle is certified infeasible.
2. Chrono tire telemetry showing a high-slip force/yaw wrench outside the
   empirically calibrated pre-slip force envelope at matched normal and
   longitudinal load.
3. A full-capacity pre-slip controller whose admissible yaw/force set is truly
   smaller than the high-slip set, rather than conservatively clipped.
4. A scenario requiring terminal yaw, a rotated corridor, split-mu, moving
   obstacles, or slide-only direct-yaw authority. These are out of the primary
   theorem scope and require separate propositions.

## Permitted final claims

### Current experimental disposition (M3265-M3273)

The pre-slip side now has two distinct evidence layers:

- M3269's continuous-search route remained inconclusive because required-slide
  optimizer recall failed at some cells. Finite comparisons nevertheless
  favored grip by `3.9-6.8 m`, and no slide counterexample appeared.
- M3270 removed optimizer recall from the registered finite scope by
  exhaustively replaying all 20 unique M3267/M3269 source actions. It completed
  `480/480` classifications and `60/60` exact replays. All `24/24` fresh seeds
  had finite grip/required-slide/free boundaries; grip was better by
  `4.0-7.5 m`, and every free optimum was grip-like.

M3270 is a finite-library/finite-cell detailed Chrono certificate. It is
independent numerical support for Theorem 1, but it does not enumerate the
continuous detailed-model control space and does not overwrite M3269.

The post-slip strictness search returned no admissible witness:

- M3271 used correct nested controls but direct body-state reset produced only
  `0.00136 rad` rear slip at `|beta|=0.8`; the state was tire-inconsistent.
- M3272 branched continuously from the frozen Chrono slide-entry history. Four
  of five branches were valid slides, but zero-steer throttle or braking was
  best at every branch and added steering gained `0.00 s`.
- M3273 tested nine deeper, continuously reached compact-model branches across
  three friction tiers. All nine were valid deep slides, but baseline and
  expanded recovery were both `0/9`.

Thus Proposition 1's weak inclusion is proved by control-set nesting, while
strict post-slip inclusion is **not empirically admitted**. The experiments
show that slip is not sufficient for steering-based drift management to add
recovery value: moderate slides may recover longitudinally, and deeper slides
may already be outside both tested kernels.

- **Admitted theory claim:** Under A1-A8, required deliberate pre-slip drift
  cannot enlarge the force-envelope avoidance kernel.
- **Admitted numerical claim:** On the frozen M3270 Chrono cells, 20-action
  library, and 0.1 m distance grid, grip `D*` was lower on all 24 fresh seeds by
  `4.0-7.5 m`; each grip boundary therefore supplies a finite-domain grip-only
  witness.
- **Admitted post-slip claim:** Expanding a control set weakly expands its
  recovery kernel. Strictness remains conditional and was not observed in
  M3271-M3273.
- **Plain-language conclusion:** Before slip, deliberate drift is unnecessary
  within the stated scope. After slip, additional drift-management authority
  may become relevant, but existing slip is neither a sufficient condition nor
  evidence that it is needed.
- **Forbidden:** a universal strict post-slip claim; detailed Chrono continuous-
  control dominance; or generalization to arbitrary vehicles, tire models,
  split-mu roads, obstacle motion, terminal poses, or slide-only actuators.
